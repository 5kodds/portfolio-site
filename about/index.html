ABOUT / OLASENI OTUSANYA

A builder interested in how ideas become useful systems.

My work sits at the intersection of technology, business and the real economy, with Africa as a starting point and the world as the horizon.

THE THROUGH-LINE

Different industries. The same question: what does it take to make a system actually work?

I have never fit neatly into one professional box. I have worked across product development, operations, digital systems, web, automation, entrepreneurship and physical businesses. I have also spent time thinking about sectors such as energy, agriculture and hospitality, not as disconnected interests, but as places where systems, incentives and execution become visible.

The recurring pattern is simple: I am drawn to complicated problems, especially the ones sitting between a good idea and reliable execution. I like understanding how the pieces fit together, where friction lives, and what a better operating model could look like.

Artificial intelligence has become an important part of that work because it changes what individuals and small teams can build. But I am less interested in AI as spectacle than in what it makes possible: better products, faster learning, more capable operators and new ways to organize work.

That interest also creates a second question: as machines become more capable, what remains distinctly human? My writing explores judgment, intent, creativity, responsibility and agency, the capacities that determine not only whether something can be built, but whether it should be built and what it is ultimately for.

I build from Africa with a global perspective. That means looking seriously at the region’s infrastructure gaps, fragmented markets and operational constraints while refusing to treat them only as limitations. They are also design conditions, and often the source of unusually valuable opportunities.
