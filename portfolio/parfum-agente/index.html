Parfum Agente: designing a better way to discover your scent.

01 THE PRODUCT HYPOTHESIS

Fragrance choice is personal, but discovery is often a wall of products.

Shoppers are asked to choose among brands, notes, seasons, occasions and unfamiliar fragrance families. My hypothesis was that a short, thoughtful consultation could translate personal preferences into useful discovery guidance before a customer knew specific perfume names.

The product promise was simple: Your scent, translated. The key design question became how to make recommendations understandable and testable instead of presenting unexplained scores as certainty.

02 THE MVP DECISIONS
01

Ask enough to learn, but not enough to exhaust.

The consultation uses 12 questions to capture preference signals. Users can move backward, see progress, retry and view the result rather than navigating a traditional product catalog first.

02

Translate answers into a usable preference model.

A six-dimensional Scent DNA and an archetype make the preference profile explicit. The local engine combines preference dimensions with fragrance notes, families, occasions and exploration signals to rank matches.

03

Choose explainable rules before a trained model.

The current matcher is rule-based. That keeps recommendations inspectable and avoids a misleading claim that the app has a trained AI personalization model. The curated catalog currently contains 31 fragrance entries.

04

Keep the experience usable when data providers fail.

The static frontend can calculate matches locally without an external API. An optional Cloudflare Worker is designed for external fragrance and commerce enrichment with keys kept off the client. Provider integration and live reliability still require deployment validation.

03 TRADE-OFFS & ENGINEERING
PRIVACY VS ANALYTICS

Local-first by default.

Preference scoring runs in the browser. Product analytics and privacy disclosures remain outstanding before a broader production launch.

EXPLAINABILITY VS SOPHISTICATION

Rules before model complexity.

A transparent scoring engine can be benchmarked with expert-reviewed profiles before investing in an opaque learning system.

FRESHNESS VS RELIABILITY

Separate curated from live.

Provider results need validation, normalization and provenance before their scores can be treated as equivalent to the curated catalog.

04 EVIDENCE, LIMITS & NEXT EXPERIMENT

A working recommendation experience, not yet proof of recommendation quality.

The repository documents a functioning consultation, local recommendation logic, automated tests, a deployable static frontend and an optional provider architecture. The V2.3 roadmap explicitly describes this as a testable public beta, not a fully production-validated recommendation business.

The next test is behavioral, not visual: run moderated sessions across fragrance-experience levels, benchmark top matches against expert-reviewed profiles, measure quiz completion, and gather qualitative feedback without capturing answers by default.

No claims of user growth, conversion lift, model accuracy or affiliate revenue are made because those results have not been established.
